The honest odds

Start with the math nobody puts in their LinkedIn carousel. If everything about a cold email is right (you’re messaging the right person, you’ve written the right email, and it comes from a domain that actually reaches the inbox), somewhere between 0.5% and 1% of people reply.

That’s one reply for every 100 to 200 emails. And a reply isn’t a meeting. It’s just a reply.

So outbound in 2026 shouldn’t be a heroic effort. It should be a machine: you build it once, let it run, and come back every few weeks to tune it.

Cold email: build the machine

People fuss a lot about leads: where to get them, which tool, which list. The infrastructure gets ignored. From talking to a lot of people in this space, most don’t actually know how to set it up.

You don’t send cold email from your main domain. You buy a handful of separate domains, create a few sender inboxes on each, and warm them up before they send a single real email. Two rules of thumb:

1

Up to 5 inboxes per domain

3 is the safe default. Most teams start with 3 to 5 domains.

2

About 45 emails per inbox, per day

Push past that and more of what you send lands in spam.

With five domains you get 15 inboxes, 675 emails a day and 3,375 a week on weekdays. At a 0.5–1% reply rate, that’s roughly 17 to 34 replies a week from a machine that runs while you do other things.

Setting it up

  1. Buy the domains. I use Porkbun. It’s cheap, and the copywriting on the site is genuinely fun.
  2. Warm them up. Warming is a whole process, but you don’t have to do it yourself. I use Premium Inboxes. Expect about three weeks before an inbox is usable.
  3. Connect them to Smartlead. Add the warmed inboxes as sender accounts, build your campaigns and start sending.1
